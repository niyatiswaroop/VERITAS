The Great Wall of China is a series of fortifications built across the historical northern borders of ancient Chinese states and Imperial China as protection against nomadic groups from the Eurasian Steppe.
Several walls were built from as early as the 7th century BC, with selective stretches later joined together by Qin Shi Huang, the first emperor of China, around 220 BC.
Little of the Qin wall remains. Most of the existing wall was built during the Ming dynasty, from 1368 to 1644.
The best-known sections of the wall were constructed of brick and stone. Estimates of its total length vary, with a commonly cited figure of about 21,196 kilometres (13,171 miles) for all branches combined.
The wall runs roughly from Dandong in the east to Lop Nur in the west, along an arc that traces the southern edge of Inner Mongolia. It is not a single continuous line but many discontinuous segments.
Contrary to a popular myth, the Great Wall is not visible to the naked eye from the Moon.
